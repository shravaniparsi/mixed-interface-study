# Closed historical cohort: contribution assessment

The frozen six-lead feasibility cohort is finished. It does not yet support a Q1-level research contribution. This is an assessment of the evidence and novelty gap, not a prediction of a journal decision.

The 36 Tiller/Iris executions are two known mechanisms, not 36 independent defects. The eight Fast Dash observations form a separate, partially qualified historical calibration. Vikunja qualification probes and the eight unexecuted TestPlanIt plans are excluded from those counts. None changes the completed independent-input study: 44 scientific paired executions, zero incremental native-only detections and zero confirmed natural defects.

## What the evidence supports

Schema acceptance, reported operation success and persistence of the requested edit can coexist with unwanted changes to other state. The Tiller/Iris common exporter demonstrates that one simple frame implementation can reconstruct those known manifestations when a person supplies the intended assignments and exceptions. Iris interventions distinguish an adapter omission from a backend replacement interaction. Fast Dash shows why a repair should be checked through both readback and subsequent invocation: a null-looking readback does not guarantee that the callback receives null.

These are credible, executable observations. They do not establish a new detection algorithm. Complete manual state contracts catch the observed violations. Related Fast Dash reports already discuss default leakage and incomplete dependency repairs. The independently selected cohort supplied no incremental advantage. Historical issue/patch selection makes this corpus unsuitable for population prevalence or held-out accuracy estimates.

## Utility gate

The prospective reuse task was repair validation that retains intended clears and cascades. The cohort supplies examples and preserved traces for that task. It does not establish that a researcher can apply one portable runner or automatically infer a sufficiently complete contract on an unseen project. The common export currently covers only the two target-row calibrations; Fast Dash requires process-state and callback observations. The remaining candidates expose contract ambiguity, authorization setup and launcher fragility. Retrospective code size or assistant time is not a valid estimate of human specification effort.

Compared with the verified neighboring work summarized in the benchmark-scope note, historical repairs and explicit persistence traces may be useful artifact features. Features alone do not demonstrate scientific novelty or superior usefulness. No external-facing task comparison, held-out method evaluation or independent artifact reuse has been completed. Therefore the benchmark-utility gate remains open and submission readiness fails.

## Next research decision

Do not expand this closed issue-selected cohort or repeat its variants to obtain larger counts. Do not draft a Results section asserting automated detection efficacy. The next substantive step must be a method or reuse hypothesis with a falsifiable advantage over complete manual contracts and existing test approaches. A plausible direction is reducing contract-specification work while retaining checks for intentional changes, but this is a proposal, not a contribution established by the current data.

Before further application runs, specify the method's inputs, what information the baselines receive, a measured effort/coverage tradeoff, failure criteria, and an independent selection procedure that does not use known issue outcomes. The current cases may be development fixtures only. If that hypothesis cannot be made distinct from existing oracle-inference work, stop this research line rather than relabel its current evidence Q1-worthy. No new experiment or spending is registered by this review.
